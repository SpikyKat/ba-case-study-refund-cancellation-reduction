# Refund and Cancellation Reduction: Business Analysis Case Study

> **Fictional case, synthetic data.** Plateful is an invented food-delivery marketplace. The data was generated by a script (`scripts/generate_orders.py`, seed 11) with assumptions in [`docs/assumptions_and_data.md`](docs/assumptions_and_data.md). Stakeholder needs are hypotheses I would validate in interviews. Nothing here comes from a real company or employer.

## The case in one paragraph
Plateful approves small refunds automatically and sends everything else to a manual queue. In a six-month sample of 10,000 orders, 60% of refund cases went to manual review, the median decision took 14.5 hours, and half the customers waiting over 24 hours contacted support again. 18% of cancellations had no reason recorded. This pack shows how a business analyst would take that from problem to rollout: scope, stakeholders, process redesign, requirements, user stories, UAT, a benefits case tested on data, and a pilot plan.

## Key findings (synthetic data)
- 623 refund cases (6.8% of delivered orders); refunds paid are 2.28% of order value.
- Late delivery drives 39% of refund value; the request rate jumps from about 4% to 18% once an order is more than 10 minutes late.
- The top 5% of customers account for 56% of refund value requested.
- 68% of cancellations happen after food is prepared or on the way.

## Recommendation
Pilot a **15 limit with at most 1 prior refund in 90 days** for auto-approval, with an 8-hour timer on the manual queue, status messages to customers and mandatory cancellation reasons. On the sample, this moves 33% of manual cases to automatic, saves about 38 agent hours, and adds at most 232 in refund value (inside a +0.2 point guardrail); net about **+53 per 1,000 orders**. A fixed 3-unit credit for late orders was tested and **rejected**: it costs more than it saves in every scenario.

Run the pilot in 3 cities with matched controls and compare changes in each group, because a plain before/after cannot separate the change from seasonality.

## Data and code
- `data/orders.csv`: 10,000 synthetic orders with cancellations, refunds, decisions and agent effort (dictionary in `docs/assumptions_and_data.md`).
- `scripts/generate_orders.py`, `scripts/analyse.py`, `scripts/build_word_pack.py`; `analysis/` holds result tables, `results.json` and four charts.
- Rebuild: `powershell -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File run_all.ps1` (Python 3.11+).

## Limitations
Synthetic data and assumed costs (agent cost 20 per hour, 60% food loss); no retention effects modelled; the leakage figure is a ceiling that assumes every previously denied refund would now be paid; targets are proposals; the pilot size depends on real volume.
